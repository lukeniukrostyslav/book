# BOOK 1 — OPENING VOICE TEST

Status: FOUNDATION 0.6 — PILOT DRAFT

## Purpose
Test the prose voice before committing to the full manuscript.

## Opening strategy
The story begins inside Taren's restoration workshop, while he is already working on a concrete object. No prologue and no world-history exposition.

The first chapter must establish:
- Taren as a skilled but ordinary professional;
- his habit of verifying before acting;
- a physical object with a traceable history;
- a small, understandable work problem;
- the first impossible relation;
- a reason to continue into Chapter 2.

This follows current opening-craft guidance: start with a character pursuing a goal under resistance, reveal worldbuilding through the scene, and make the chapter ending create a concrete next question rather than relying on exposition. citeturn0search7turn0search0

## Voice target
- restrained, intelligent, sensory;
- no purple prose;
- no generic medieval-fantasy vocabulary;
- dry humor through character behavior;
- mystery emerges from precise observation;
- emotional information appears through action, not explanation;
- dialogue should sound contemporary in clarity without sounding modern in slang.

## Pilot prose

Taren knew a damaged hinge by the sound it made.

This one complained before he touched it.

He stopped with the cloth halfway across the brass plate and listened.

A faint click came from the workbench.

Then another.

Nothing moved.

Taren set the cloth down.

The workshop was quiet enough that he could hear the river carts outside on the street below, wheels knocking over the same three stones they had been knocking over for years. Somewhere in the back room, his father was arguing with a customer about the difference between old wood and neglected wood.

Taren ignored both sounds.

The hinge was not moving. He checked it anyway.

Two screws. One bent pin. A hairline fracture near the lower plate.

Ordinary.

He turned the piece over.

The Trace was wrong.

Taren stared at it for a moment.

Not weak. Not damaged. Wrong.

He brought the inspection lamp closer and wiped the metal again, although the brass was already clean enough to show his reflection.

The relation should have ended at the hinge.

It did not.

Something in the lower plate was still answering a second point on the object.

Taren looked at the point.

There was nothing there.

He checked the join.

Nothing.

He checked the repair marks.

Nothing.

He checked the object's record card.

Nothing.

"That's inconvenient," he said.

His father called from the back room.

"What is?"

"Nothing."

"Then why are you talking to it?"

Taren did not answer.

He reached for the second inspection lens.

The Trace remained.

Two things that had never touched were registering a relation as if they had.

That was not supposed to happen.

He wrote the date in the margin of the record card.

Then, beneath it, he wrote one sentence:

**Verify before assuming.**

He looked at the hinge again.

For the first time that morning, he did not know what he was looking at.

## Pilot QA

### Character
PASS — Taren is introduced through professional behavior, not biography.

### Goal
PASS — he is repairing/verifying a hinge.

### Visible pressure
PASS — the object presents an anomaly that challenges his professional model.

### Worldbuilding
PASS — Trace is demonstrated rather than explained.

### Mystery
PASS — the impossible relation is visible and concrete.

### Exposition
PASS — no history lecture, no cosmology dump.

### Voice
PASS provisionally — restrained and precise; full assessment requires Ch1 completion.

### Chapter-end pull
PASS — the reader needs to know whether the anomaly survives another test.

### Originality check
PASS — no chosen-one signal, prophecy, ancient-god reveal, school, dragon, fae, magical artifact destiny, or generic fantasy exposition.
