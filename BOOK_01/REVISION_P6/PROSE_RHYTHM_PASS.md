# P6 — PROSE / RHYTHM PASS

## Status
COMPLETE

## Scope
Whole Book 1 prose/rhythm audit, with corrective patterns applied to the current revision layer.

## Editorial rules
- Cut redundant restatement where the reader already has the information.
- Vary sentence length around investigation, dialogue, action, and reflection.
- Avoid repetitive physical beats.
- Prefer concrete verbs over weak explanatory constructions.
- Preserve the deliberately restrained voice; do not inflate prose into decorative fantasy language.
- Keep short sentences for pressure and discovery, but avoid making every paragraph equally clipped.
- Preserve uncertainty without repeatedly naming it.
- Keep dialogue economical and character-specific.

A sentence-level revision should improve clarity, flow, tone, precision, and concision without erasing the author's voice; repetition is useful when it creates rhythm or emphasis, but accidental restatement should be removed. citeturn0search0turn0search2turn0search6

## Global findings and actions

### 1. Sentence rhythm
Problem:
The investigation chapters sometimes stack several short declarative sentences in sequence, producing a mechanically even pulse.

Action:
- Keep short sentences at discoveries, danger, decisions.
- Join adjacent factual statements when they belong to one observation.
- Use longer analytical sentences when Taren is comparing evidence.
- Allow paragraph rhythm to change between archive scenes and field scenes.

Result: PASS.

### 2. Repeated physical beats
Problem:
Repeated patterns such as looking, nodding, putting down a page, and checking a map can become invisible filler.

Action:
- Retain only beats that reveal attention, emotion, or decision.
- Replace neutral gestures with task-specific action.
- Remove duplicate reaction beats.

Result: PASS.

### 3. Explanatory repetition
Problem:
The manuscript intentionally distinguishes fact / hypothesis / unknown, but repeated labels can sound procedural if every scene states them identically.

Action:
- Use explicit labels when they are part of the investigation.
- Elsewhere show the distinction through what the characters do.
- Do not repeat a conclusion after the evidence already made it clear.

Result: PASS.

### 4. Taren's prose
Target:
Precise, restrained, analytical.

Correction:
Avoid giving him poetic certainty when the character would instead describe evidence. His strongest emotional moments remain simple.

Result: PASS.

### 5. Nera's prose/dialogue
Target:
Fast, concrete, practical.

Correction:
Keep her sentences shorter during operational scenes, but allow pauses when she is reconsidering action.

Result: PASS.

### 6. Edran's prose/dialogue
Target:
Controlled, formal, compressed.

Correction:
His restraint remains visible through omission and carefully bounded statements, rather than repeated mysterious phrasing.

Result: PASS.

### 7. Mystery language
Removed/limited:
- repeated "we don't know" statements
- repeated "this proves nothing" statements
- repeated "the model is incomplete" statements

Replaced where appropriate with:
- tests
- contradictions
- changed decisions
- incomplete records
- observed consequences

Result: PASS.

### 8. Action vs explanation
Physical scenes now prioritize:
1. immediate physical fact
2. character response
3. decision
4. interpretation afterward

Result: PASS.

### 9. Chapter-end rhythm
Chapter endings should either:
- create a new question,
- change the interpretation of existing evidence,
- force a decision,
- or create controlled anticipation.

Result: PASS.

### 10. Final sentence discipline
The final lines are kept strong and sparse. No extra explanation is added after the Chapter 32 conclusion.

Result: PASS.
